A company in mid-transformation. Axiscades is selling the engineering-services business that built it (about 60% of FY26 revenue) to Akkodis and using the cash to become a defence electronics, aerospace manufacturing and space company. The remaining business is growing fast but is not yet profitable after interest and depreciation, while the promoter has sold about 13% of the company in four months. Its "Power 930" plan targets ₹9,000 cr revenue and ₹960 cr PAT by FY30.

CH-01

Industry & value-chain position

After the divestment, Axiscades will sit in three industries: Indian defence electronics, aerospace manufacturing and supply chain, and embedded computing for AI hardware. In all three it is a supplier to bigger "prime" contractors, not the platform maker itself.

Market backdrop
  • Defence budget FY27: about ₹7.85 lakh cr, roughly 15% above FY26, after Operation Sindoor, with a push toward ~2% of GDP and higher modernisation capex.
  • Import substitution: India aims to cut imports from roughly 70% of procurement to under 30%. Positive indigenisation lists cover 300+ items, including radar subsystems, avionics and mission computers, which are Mistral's product lines.
  • A&D market: India's aerospace and defence market is estimated at USD 26.8 bn (2023) growing to USD 48.4 bn by 2032 (~6.8% CAGR), per Investorstack.
  • Civil aerospace: Airbus and Boeing hold a combined backlog above 15,000 aircraft, which drives demand for offset-linked parts, harnesses and MRO in India.
  • Defence pipeline: management says its addressable pipeline in unmanned systems, missiles, radar and electronic warfare exceeds ₹24,000 cr.
Why requalification is the moat

Design wins last decades

  • Defence electronics take 3–7 years from design to qualification and then stay in production for 15–30 years. A supplier embedded on a platform (Tejas, Su-30 upgrade, Ashwini radar) is hard to displace because requalifying a replacement takes 2–4 years.
  • Certifications held: DO-254, DO-178 (airborne hardware and software), MIL-STD, CEMILAC. Cloud Wave brings AS9100D for aerospace parts.
  • Margin ladder cited by management: 25–40% EBITDA on defence production versus 12–15% on engineering services; prototype work can be single-digit or negative.

Radars send out radio pulses and read the echoes. Axiscades supplies the processing electronics that turn raw echoes into tracks, and is building hangars to integrate whole radar systems. The Ashwini low-level transportable radar (LLTR) finds low-flying aircraft and drones.

Before a missile is loaded on a jet, a test bench checks every circuit. Axiscades has built these for MBDA since the MICA programme and is moving into launcher systems. It is also developing an RF seeker (the missile's "eye") whose prototype for BrahMos-NG the customer has endorsed, and a 30 kW mobile laser weapon.

Final applicationAir-to-air and cruise missiles for the Indian forces and MBDA programmes; Project Kusha (indigenous long-range air defence) work is deepening per management.

CH-03

Promoter: history & skin in the game

Who controls the company
  • The sole promoter entity is Jupiter Capital Pvt Ltd, a Bengaluru investment house associated with Rajeev Chandrasekhar. It is a financial owner rather than an operating founder.
  • Operations are run by professional managers: Alfonso Martinez Fernandez, Group CEO & MD since January 2025 (after Arun Krishnamurthi resigned), and Dr Sampath Ravinarayanan, Chairman since January 2025.
  • Defence is led through Mistral Solutions, acquired in two phases (2017–19). The defence and ACAT units were merged into Mistral in December 2024.
The key fact for this section

The promoter is selling, not buying. Jupiter sold 10 lakh shares on 30 June 2026 and 46.2 lakh shares (10.86%) on 28–29 Sep 2026, the latter as a block of ~₹830 cr at about ₹1,824 a share. Kotak MF (4.89%), Abakkus (3.03%) and Svadha were the buyers. This came days before the Phase 1 closing.

CH-06

Is there a J-curve?

Yes, and the company is near the bottom of it · schematic, not a forecast

Shape drawn from management statements; positions are illustrative. Reported FY27 profit will be inflated by the ~₹1,255 cr sale gain, which hides the dip in the operating business.

Evidence for the dip
  • Continuing operations excluding XiDA did not cover Q1 finance cost (₹8.9 cr) and depreciation (₹8.8 cr).
  • Aerospace manufacturing ran ₹12.9 cr of costs on ₹6.1 cr revenue in Q1.
  • The sold business earned the group's best segment margin (engineering services ~26% in FY26).
  • Organic investment plan of ~₹1,600 cr (Arihant) across Devanahalli and Hyderabad; net debt rose from ₹11 cr (Mar 2025) to ₹186 cr (Mar 2026), plus ₹200 cr NCDs in Sep 2026.
Evidence for the upswing
  • Defence revenue doubled YoY in Q1; assured visibility of ₹4,557 cr is about 25x Q1 continuing revenue.
  • Production contracts carry 25–40% EBITDA per management; Cloud Wave and the target acquisition are already profitable.
  • Up to ~₹2,250 cr of sale proceeds can retire debt and fund capex without new equity.
Verdict

A genuine J-curve: profitable services are being swapped for a manufacturing build-out whose returns arrive from FY28. The curve's depth depends on Tranche 1 cash arriving in Q3 FY27, and its upswing on defence programmes (Tejas, MAC, seeker) staying on schedule.
