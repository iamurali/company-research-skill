A 54-year-old, family-run Meerut transformer maker that spent FY24 paying for short-circuit certifications, and is now cashing that ticket: it is one of five Indian makers certified for 500 MVA / 400 kV units and one of two for 100 MVA / 220 kV Scott railway transformers. 400 kV units were 70% of transformer sales in Q1 FY27. Management guides ₹950 cr revenue for FY27 at FY26-like margins, and is doubling capacity for FY29.

BAY 01

Industry & where Kanohar sits

A transformer works like a pressure valve for electricity: it raises voltage so power can travel hundreds of kilometres with little loss, then lowers it so homes, factories and trains can use it. Every new substation, renewable park and electrified rail line needs one.

Why the 400 kV club is small

Certification is the moat

  • Utilities only accept extra-high-voltage units whose design has passed a short-circuit test at an accredited lab (CPRI or NHPTL). Each test is expensive and a failure costs a unit.
  • Kanohar spent ₹5.22 cr on testing in FY24 and has short-circuit tested 200+ ratings. That campaign directly won the ₹568.67 cr POWERGRID 500 MVA order in June 2025, its largest ever.
  • Only 5 Indian companies hold 500 MVA/400 kV certification; only 4 are RDSO-approved for 100 MVA/132 kV Scott and 2 for 220 kV Scott.
  • Management on the Q1 FY27 call: industry lead times are 18–24 months, so tenders being bid now are for FY29 delivery.

The large oil-filled machines that sit at grid substations. Kanohar's biggest are 500 MVA, 400/220/33 kV auto-transformers, short-circuit tested at NHPTL in 2023. These link the 400 kV inter-state backbone to state 220 kV networks.

Installed at traction sub-stations beside the railway track. They take power from the state grid and convert it to the single-phase 25 kV that feeds the overhead wire the train's pantograph touches.

Scott-connected transformers

100 MVArating
220 / 2×55 kVand 132 / 2×55 kV

A special winding arrangement (two transformers joined in a "T") that turns the grid's balanced three-phase supply into two single-phase outputs. That keeps the grid balanced when a heavy rail load draws power, and supports the 2×25 kV system used on high-speed and heavy-haul lines.

Final applicationDedicated Freight Corridors, high-speed rail, heavy-haul electrification. Management sizes the Scott opportunity at ~₹1,000 cr by FY30. Introduced to the portfolio in FY25.

A coil that "soaks up" excess voltage. Long 400 kV lines carrying little power (common at night on solar evacuation routes) see voltage rise dangerously; a reactor connected to the line holds it steady.

BAY 04

Government policy & tailwinds

Transmission

₹9.15 lakh cr to FY32

National Electricity Plan (Transmission): network grows from 5.00 to 6.48 lakh circuit-km; ~143,000 ckm of new lines by FY32 with 400 kV taking the biggest share. Each 400 kV substation needs multiple 315–500 MVA transformers and reactors.

Direct demand for core product
Renewables

500 GW non-fossil by 2030

India has crossed 200 GW of renewables; CEA plans ~594 GW RE by FY32. Green Energy Corridor Phase III builds evacuation lines from RE-rich states. Gujarat and Rajasthan, Kanohar's top markets, are the biggest RE evacuation states.

Reactors + 400 kV units
Railways

National Rail Plan 2030

Targets 100% electrification, freight corridors and high-speed corridors that use the 2×25 kV system, which needs Scott and auto-transformers. Management sees traction transformers as a ~₹20,000 cr opportunity over 5–10 years.

Order book share still small
Distribution

₹2.86 lakh cr, FY27–30

CEA Draft Distribution Perspective Plan 2030 and ageing-asset replacement. Low relevance for Kanohar, which has de-emphasised distribution units.

Indirect
Localisation

Make in India

Public procurement preference for domestic makers and limits on bidders from bordering countries narrow the field in PSU tenders. Global transformer shortages also lengthen lead times, which favours makers with free capacity.

Supports pricing
Contract terms

IEEMA price variation

Utility contracts carry IEEMA price-variation clauses that pass copper and CRGO movements through. CRISIL notes escalation clauses in all contracts; the RHP says 4 of 35 contracts are fixed-price.

BAY 06

Is there a J-curve?

The first J has already played out · actuals

FY24 was the bottom of the J: revenue fell 9% to ₹277 cr, cash flow was negative, ₹5.2 cr went on short-circuit tests and the plant ran at 16.5%. Those certifications then won 400 kV orders, and margin more than doubled in two years. This is a completed J, now priced in at ~60x trailing earnings.

The next two curves to watch

Curve 2 · 765 kV qualification

The same playbook one step up: spend on testing and prototype units, win a first order, then use the track record to win more. Management says it needs 3–4 years of field performance before 765 kV adds meaningful revenue. If it works, it repeats FY24→FY26 in a larger, less crowded class. Cost so far is small because Gangol is already built for 765 kV.

Curve 3 · Capacity doubling in FY29

Utilisation is guided to ~90% by FY28, then capacity nearly doubles. Management does not expect growth above 40–45% in FY29, so utilisation would fall back toward ~50–60% (derived). Fixed cost is low (depreciation was ₹3 cr in FY26), so this is a shallow dip, not a deep J.

Verdict

No large J-curve ahead with heavy near-term pain. The upside optionality is 765 kV plus high-speed rail; the visible risk is management's own warning of possible 400 kV margin pressure 3–4 years out as industry supply catches up.
